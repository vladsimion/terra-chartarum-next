Before a line is drawn, there is the ground itself: rivers that shift their courses, coastlines reshaped by erosion, mountains formed over geological ages, and climates that transform the conditions of human habitation. Earth asks the question every map must answer first: what is actually there, before anyone chooses what to keep? It brings together geology, physical geography, landscape and ecology: the ground that geographical knowledge is built on. Every map simplifies this complexity, reducing a living, changing environment to a surface that can be measured and read. Elevations become contours, wetlands become symbols, and seasonal variations disappear into averages. What is gained in legibility is lost in depth. This room returns to the physical world behind the representation, to what maps can describe but never fully contain.
