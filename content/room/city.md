When space becomes inhabited, it acquires a different kind of geography. Streets, squares, walls, parcels and addresses transform the landscape into an organised environment, shaped by the routines and relationships of those who live within it. City explores the mapping of settlements and the ways in which urban space is measured, divided and governed. The plan and the cadastre do more than describe the built environment: they establish property, define jurisdictions and make populations legible to those who govern and tax them. A street becomes an address, open ground becomes a parcel, and a neighbourhood becomes an administrative unit. Yet the ordered city on paper is never quite the city experienced by its inhabitants. Informal spaces, customary rights and everyday patterns of movement often resist the geometries imposed upon them. This room works in the gap between the lived city and the city on paper, and asks what the map does to each.
