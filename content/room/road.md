The world is not always experienced as a territory to be surveyed from above. It is also encountered through movement: along roads and rivers, across seas, between ports and through successive stages of a journey. Road explores this mobile geography, examining the routes and itineraries along which people, goods and ideas have travelled. Its maps privilege connection over enclosure, passage over possession, and relationships between places over the continuous territory that surrounds them. A merchant's understanding of the Mediterranean, for example, may be organised around harbours, sailing conditions and commercial connections rather than the political boundaries that divide its shores. The same sea can therefore appear as a network of opportunity to the traveller and as a field of strategic control to the state. Routes create their own geographies, bringing distant places into relationship while leaving much of the intervening landscape unrecorded. This room follows the lines of movement that have connected the world, asking how travel and exchange have shaped the ways in which space is known and mapped.
