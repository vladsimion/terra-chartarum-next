<h2 id="themes">Themes</h2>

The "Browse by" control lets you explore the layer catalogue in three ways. Changing the browsing mode reorganises the catalogue without changing the layers displayed on the map.

Themes describe what a layer represents. There are seven thematic categories:

- Territories & Boundaries
- Networks & Circulation
- Places & Settlements
- Names, Peoples & Attestations
- Conflict, Campaigns & Frontiers
- Cartographic Evidence
- Historical Map Overlays

Every layer belongs to exactly one theme. "Search layers" searches across all themes, regardless of the current browsing mode.

The separate "Search places" field searches for geographical locations. Selecting a result moves the map to that location and places a marker on it. Select "Remove marker" to clear it.

<h2 id="collections">Collections</h2>

A collection brings together layers developed for a particular research programme or body of work, such as the Corpus Chartarum Daciae.

Browsing by collection groups its associated layers. Select "Add default layers" to load the collection's recommended starting set onto the map in a single step.

A collection is an organisational framework, not a map layer. It groups existing layers without becoming one itself. Not every layer belongs to a collection.

<h2 id="rooms">Rooms</h2>

Rooms represent the seven intellectual approaches through which Terra Chartarum explores its material: Earth, Map, City, Border, Road, Archive and Theatre.

A layer is associated with a room only when it is used by at least one published essay assigned to that room. This relationship connects the Atlas to the site's editorial and research structure. Layers not yet used in a published essay appear under "In no room".

As new essays are published, layers acquire room associations through their use in those essays. A layer may therefore appear in more than one room if it supports essays in different intellectual contexts.
