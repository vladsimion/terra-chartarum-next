<h2 id="time-control">The time control</h2>

The Atlas displays the world at a selected point in time. Enter a year in the "Year" field or drag the adjacent slider to move through the chronology. For dates before the Common Era, type a minus sign, the ordinary hyphen key (for example, -106 for 106 BC).

The slider spans the earliest and latest dates covered by any layer in the Atlas. When the Atlas first opens, the selected year is set to the midpoint of the date range covered by the default collection's layers.

A feature appears whenever the selected year falls within the range in which it could have existed. Some layers carry dates for individual features; others are dated as a whole. The inspector identifies which dating method applies to each layer.

<h2 id="uncertain-dates">Uncertain dates</h2>

Historical dates are rarely exact. A feature may be dated to a range, described as approximate, or assigned only to a broader period: about 106, before 271, or sometime in the third century.

The Atlas distinguishes between what the evidence establishes and what it merely allows. A feature is drawn at full strength during the years in which all plausible readings of its date agree. Where its presence is possible but not certain, it appears in a paler tone. The legend identifies it as "Date uncertain", and the inspector explains the uncertainty.

Paler does not mean less important, or that the feature itself is in doubt. It means that the available evidence permits the selected year but cannot establish it with certainty.

<h2 id="out-of-range">Out of range</h2>

Each layer also has an overall date range. When you move beyond that period, the layer remains in "Active layers" and is marked "Out of range", rather than disappearing. This preserves your selected layers as you move through the chronology, even when they have no features to display at the current year.

To narrow the catalogue to layers covering the selected year, tick "Only show layers relevant to this year" above the catalogue. This filters the catalogue, not your active layers: anything you have already added remains selected.
