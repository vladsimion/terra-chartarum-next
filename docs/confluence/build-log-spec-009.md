The one decision slice 3 forced. The contract said a native range input would drive the reveal through a CSS custom property with no script. CSS cannot read a range input's value, so without a script the reveal would never have moved. The editor chose a native labelled radio group of five reveal positions (0, 25, 50, 75, 100 %): the arrow keys move the reveal, it works with script off, and a browser without `:has()` gets the two figures in full over a plain form. FR-007, research.md and the components contract were amended in the same pull request; Home and End, which a radio group does not have, left the test.

The one decision slice 4 forced. The legacy squeeze was a stack of CSS filters, and the shell's lint forbids `filter` outright. The squeeze is now the tokens themselves: the gallery rewrites four `--plate-*` custom properties that every plate reads, so ink and paper exchange places and the relief is a real negative in both colour schemes, with no new colour token and no filter.

The two things the port itself forced. First, provenance: the nine stone locators were written as `proposed_by: ai` and the author corrected it, so all thirteen evidence records now read `proposed_by: person`. The field is an enumeration of `person` and `ai`, so it records the kind of proposer and not a name; a person's name belongs in `locator.verified_by`, and only once that person has read the source. All thirteen stay `pending`, so what will hold them at Publish is the rule that a verified locator needs a verifier, a date and the copy consulted. Second, the drawings: moving them onto tokens grew the plate vocabulary twice, to the eight classes the first six stones needed and then to `plate-rule-muted` and `plate-paper-rule` for the Josephine plane-table grid and the Russian scale bar. One change of that move is on the ledger for the author to rule on (L21): an upright Garamond place-name becomes italic, because the vocabulary has three hands and none of them is an upright body hand.

The three things the rest of the port forced. The compare slider is a radio group and takes no caption props, so the two figure labels became their figures' captions and the legacy caption the paragraph beneath, words unchanged, and the invitation to drag the seam became an invitation to move it. The scrollytelling has no control, so the core sample's label no longer tells the reader to select a stratum. And the 52 pit cells are data now, in `content/essay/essay-000001.cells.json`: a JSON string carries no emphasis, so `extra muros`, `absent` and the two `(uncertain)` readings keep their words and lose their slant, while each table gains the reading column the vocabulary will fill and the corpus column that marks every row not yet corpus-backed.

One gap is visible in the page and will stay until the author closes it. The third radar, the secret century against the satellite, has its heading, its chart and its prose but no interaction frame, because the fifth declaration cannot be written until the firing-plan sheet is identified and the framework refuses an interaction id the record does not declare.

**The decision sheet, and what answering it did.** The work left before Publish was spread over thirty-eight items in four files, which is why it kept reading as a wall. One sheet, `docs/review/anchor-essay-decisions.md`, collected all of it with a proposed answer to each, and the author answered it in one line on 18 November. Reading the eighty-eight readiness errors properly had already moved eight of them off him entirely: four held cartographers and four held bibliography entries were a short release list, which research R10 foresaw, and five of the six maps missing attribution evidence could point at evidence records that already targeted them.

The sheet also shrank the one blocker that had held the essay at Research. FR-006 wants five interactions and only four could be declared, because the firing-plan pointer was called unidentified. But the essay's own stone text gives the series, the scale and the year, and the appendix names the georeferencing, which is the same series-level pointer already accepted for two other stones. On that basis the fifth interaction was declared and Outline opened. Its question and interpretation had to be written for the declaration to be valid, so they are adapted from the author's prose and recorded as his to confirm.

Two of the sheet's own proposals were to refuse. L21 asked the author to reject it, so rejecting it is what "as proposed" meant: `StonePlate` gained a fourth hand and the eleven upright Garamond groups moved onto it, so his letterforms stand as he drew them. L07 and one reconciliation row carried no proposal and stay open; a pending ledger row blocks Build, so L07 is now what holds the next promotion.

**What the cell vocabulary settled.** The 52 cells took the corpus's own attestation classes, so a cell and the attestation that will one day back it line up without a translation step. Filling it did most of the reconciliation's work by itself: the four cells that read `absent` under Vesconte now read `extra_muros`, as the corpus does and as the essay's prose for those cells implies, the marks left the name column for the reading column, and the differences fell from seven to one. One row the sheet never asked about turned up on the way, where taking the corpus's class would have erased a hedge the author wrote; the hedge was kept.

**The essay is published.** On 18 November the author answered the decision sheet in one line, and the thirty-eight decisions it held carried the essay from Research through Outline, Draft, Build and Design QA to Publish in a day. He read the page in both colour schemes from rendered screenshots rather than a full design read, and signed the three sign-offs through this session on his explicit instruction: the fields carry his name and that date, which is his signature, not a judgement made for him.

Fifty records went out with the essay and nothing else — its fourteen evidence records, the ten sources behind them, their fourteen bibliography entries, the six maps it shows and those maps' five makers — and a test written before the release pins that from both sides, so nothing of the held import can leak out with a later essay unnoticed. The leak scan over the real production build reports none.

**What running the budgets against a real page found.** The fixtures had been passing for weeks; the first run against fifteen thousand words and sixteen drawings scored 0.95 on accessibility and missed the paint budget by 59 milliseconds. Three faults axe had not raised: thirteen stratum headings two levels below their section, thirteen links whose accessible names did not contain their visible text, and thirteen timeline events that overlap on a phone, which fails the target-size rule however large each link is. The paint budget was the cost of laying out sixteen inline drawings on a throttled mobile CPU, answered by skipping the offscreen ones. None of this was visible on a fixture, which is the argument for building the real page early rather than at the end.

State after T062: 2769 Vitest tests in 228 files, all passing; `typecheck` and `lint:css` clean; the integrity gate green at 0 errors; all thirty-one ledger rows ruled on and every reconciliation difference resolved.
