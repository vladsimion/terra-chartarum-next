# Review of the competing hypotheses register (TCX-78)

Prepared on 23 November 2026 for the author's three open questions in `hypotheses.md` ("Open for the author"). These are **proposals**: `hypotheses.md` is unchanged, and each item below is the author's to accept, change or reject. Nothing here is a finding, and nothing has been graded.

The programme's named tests ("Quantitative methods") are abbreviated as follows:

| Code | Test | Data |
|---|---|---|
| **T-ON** | Share of native Dacian names in inscriptions | EDCS, EDH, IDR |

## Question 3 first: should H1.4 stand on its own?

**Recommendation: keep H1.4 separate, and sharpen how it differs from H1.3.**

- The two make different predictions. H1.3 is a two-bank zone, with movement across the Danube in both directions. H1.4 is a north-only story: Romance speech survives on the margins, and the basin is resettled from those margins, not from the Balkans.
- They are weakened by different results. A Balkan origin for the basin's earliest Romanian place-names weakens H1.4 but not H1.3. Folded together, H1.3 would absorb results that point in opposite directions, which is the advantage the register says the cautious middle must not get.
- **Proposed sharpening for H1.4:**
  - Predicts that the earliest Romanian place-names and the earliest Vlach mentions in the basin cluster nearest the margins (Banat, Hațeg, Făgăraș, Maramureș) and spread inward over time (T-PN, mapped).
  - Weakened by the basin's earliest Romanian names showing no gradient from the margins, or by its contact features pointing to the Balkans rather than to the northern margins.

## Question 1: is any hypothesis missing?

These are candidates a specialist might expect to see. None is asserted as true. Add one, merge it or reject it. If one is rejected, the register can say so, so that a reader sees it was considered.

1. **Q1: a mobile pastoral population (candidate H1.5).**
   - **Claim:** a Romance-speaking population stayed north of the Danube but was largely transhumant, so it left a thin settlement record.
   - **Predicts:** a weak T-SD signal in the uplands alongside Latin pastoral vocabulary, and early mentions of Vlachs as herders with customary law (*ius valachicum*).
   - **Weakened by:** upland survey finding the seasonal sites the model needs absent where survey intensity was high.
   - **Why it matters:** without it, an empty T-SD result counts against H1.1 even where low archaeological visibility is the expected outcome.
   - **Overlap:** it could also be a variant of H1.1 or H1.4. The register already notes that silence is weak evidence, and this hypothesis gives that rule a testable form.
2. **Q1: continued Roman presence on the north bank (a variant, not a new hypothesis).** Late Roman bridgeheads on the north bank in the 4th to 6th centuries could have kept Romance speech alive near the Danube. This bears on H1.1 and H1.3. Proposal: name it as a variant under H1.3, which predicts Roman-period finds after 275 concentrated near the Danube rather than in the basin (T-SD by distance from the river).
3. **Q2: distinctness through the privileged communities (candidate H2.4).**
   - **Claim:** the Saxon and Székely self-government (from the 1224 Andreanum for the Saxons) made Transylvania distinct as much as the voivodate did.
   - **Predicts:** the privileged communities' jurisdictions attested as separate from the voivode's, and the "three nations" acting together before 1541.
   - **Weakened by:** their jurisdictions being subordinate to the voivode's throughout.
   - **Overlap:** it may be enough to name it as a strand in H2.0.
4. **Q4: maps follow their information (candidate H4.4).**
   - **Claim:** accuracy and borders follow the information available to the mapmaker (itineraries, military survey) more than borders, models or patrons.
   - **Predicts:** a step change in accuracy after new survey work, regardless of publisher.
   - **Weakened by:** no change in accuracy after dated survey campaigns.
   - **Why it matters:** this is the main alternative to H4.2 that a historian of cartography would expect.
5. **Q3: a regional rather than temporal fit (an optional variant of H3.3).** The fit could vary by region (for example the Saxon Land against the counties against the Székely Land) more than by period. Proposal: measure the H3 fit index by region as well as by period. No new hypothesis is needed.
6. **Q5: no addition proposed.** The symmetry rule already requires each hypothesis to be tested on both traditions and on the Saxon and Western ones.
